# Adaptive Study Platform Brief

## Problem
A single student needs fast, exam-focused practice for Waves/Particles and Circuits—not another passive content library. They must solve on paper, explain reasoning, and identify weak concepts before imminent assessments.

## Target User and Outcome
The learner is preparing for a Waves/Particles exam on Monday 14 and a Circuits quiz on Tuesday 15 (through, but excluding, Thévenin). Success means independently solving sampled workshop problems, explaining each choice of law, and reaching a visible readiness threshold before the exam.

## Recommended MVP
Start with a browser-based guided practice session for one curated problem at a time. Import only the current workshop problems and their authored solution paths. Do not delay study with accounts, social features, or a generic course builder.

## Core Learning Loop
1. Select a problem by priority.
2. The learner submits a diagram, data, unknowns, governing law, and numbered steps after working on paper.
3. The trainer checks a structured rubric, gives the smallest useful progressive hint, and asks for correction.
4. The learner completes a similar variant and gives a short oral explanation.
5. Mastery and next-problem priority update.

## Essential Interactive Artifacts
- Circuit sketchpad for nodes, currents, polarities, and source transformations.
- Wave visualizer for amplitude, phase, wavelength, standing modes, and energy.
- Formula-and-units checklist with error-specific prompts.
- Oral-defense timer and question cards.

Visual and hands-on artifacts support engagement; VARK scores are preferences, not fixed learning limits.

## Prioritization Logic
Prioritize due date, prerequisite concepts, low mastery, recent errors, and problems not yet solved unaided. Today, Waves/Particles outranks all build work; then cover Circuits foundations before Thévenin.

## Non-Goals
No unrestricted AI answer generator, automatic grading of arbitrary handwriting, full LMS, or CAD/Calculus coursework management in the MVP.

## Risks and Assumptions
Exact correctness depends on curated problem data and an explicit answer/solution schema. Without it, feedback must be rubric-based and human-confirmed; automatic symbolic grading must not be promised. Source PDFs may need manual extraction and copyright-safe use.

## Success Criteria
A learner can complete three representative problems unaided, pass one variant per pattern, and verbally justify the method with no critical conceptual error.
